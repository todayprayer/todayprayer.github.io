Controls

← →Turn, on your feet or on your knees, even while you hold your back or head with the mouse. The camera turns with you, so what is ahead of you is always up the screen. ↑Walk straight forward, the way you are facing. From your knees, stand up. ↓Hold to walk straight back. ↓ ↓Tap twice to genuflect. When you are meant to stay down, you stay down; Ctrl or ↑ brings you up. ShiftKneel, right knee first. (Walking around St. Vitus, Shift runs instead.) CtrlStand up if you are down; sit if you are at a seat. SpaceMake the sign of the cross. 1–4Choose your spoken response. While the priest is speaking you can get it ready, and change your mind; it is said when he finishes. If you have none ready by then, you have a second and a half. If you chose to type your responses, type them like speaking: you have that same time to begin, and after that each right letter adds its share of the time the line takes to say, at four syllables a second unless you change that in the options. The Confiteor is typed whole, and your turns and strikes come due as you reach those words. Fall behind that pace for long, or stop, and you run out. It is followed as speech is heard: accents, capitals, punctuation and spelling do not matter (caeli or coeli, Jesu or Iesu), a few letters out in a word is let go, and in a longer line so is a missed or stray word. It ends by itself when you reach the end of the line. The box turns red only if you have lost the line. AltOnly while a line is waiting to be typed: your hands are busy, so Alt makes the movement that is due now: a bow, a turn, a strike of the breast, kneeling, standing, a genuflection. You still have to remember it at the right moment, and pressing it with nothing due is a slip. The sign of the cross is always Space; walking and handling things stay yours. DragAnywhere but your own body: look all around you, and up at the sky, without changing the way you walk. RCamera back behind you (or double-click). WChange the weather: clear, cloudy, overcast, rain. EscPause

Everything else you do with your own body. Grab one of the circles on your hands, head or back, with the mouse or a finger, and move it.

A handDrag it to a thing and let go: a cruet to take it, the table to put it down, the priest to hand it over or pour, the holy water, the gate, the rope, his alb or chasuble. Shake the hand with the bells to ring them. Trace your forehead, breast, left and right shoulder for the sign of the cross; forehead, lips, breast for the small crosses; bring it to your breast to strike it, once for each blow. Bring what you are holding to your lips to kiss it. Your headDrag it down to bow your head, or onto the priest’s hand to kiss it. Sideways, it turns you. Your backDrag it down to bow: a little for a moderate bow, far for a profound one. Drag it back up to straighten. Sideways, it turns you: that is how you face the priest while you are bowed.

While you hold a hand, circles mark what you can reach. They do not tell you which is wanted.

Walking around is only for fun: nothing is scored, and a Mass you start from there can never fail. Watching from the choir, a choice when you go to serve, you sit on the bench beside the altar (or, walking around, go to a bench, sit there, and start the Mass). All that is asked of you is the second Confiteor with the servers, going up to receive, and going back; only there can you slip, and the Mass goes on unless you knock someone over. As the priest (experimental, the other choice when you go to serve), you walk, turn, bow, genuflect, make the signs and say the audible prayers; his hands at the altar and his silent prayers happen by themselves.

Reminders at the top say which button comes next, and why. Turn them off with the button under the keys, or in the options.

On a phone or tablet the same moves are buttons on screen. The Mass runs in real time: the priest and the other server never wait, so it is your job to keep up. A moment you miss is a slip, facing the wrong way is a slip (nobody turns you: use ← →), and a response not given in time goes unsaid. Ten slips are allowed, a major slip counting as three; one more ends the Mass. Miss something essential (keeping with the priest in procession, what he needs brought to him, the bell at the Consecration) or walk out, and the Mass ends for you and you choose to start again. You keep your place: leaving it is a major slip, staying away ends the Mass, and so does walking up to the altar unbidden. In procession those behind you wait for you to go ahead. Everyone is solid: walking square into someone or standing in their way is a major slip, and knocking the priest (while he carries something, on the steps, by pushing, or a second time) ends the Mass. It is up to you to know what comes next: the top right shows the keys and the buttons, the warnings at the bottom right tell you what you missed, and the summary at the end explains each slip. Circles on the floor show where to go, unless you turn them off in the options.
